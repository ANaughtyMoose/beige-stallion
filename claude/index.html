0Ignition · 31 August 2018

Ignition

Every legend starts with a light on the dash.

Friday, 31 August 2018. A beige Corolla, built in the year 2000, leaves Mountain View with a new owner at the wheel.

He drives 80 miles that first day. Halfway, a light comes on. Check engine.

It stays lit for about six weeks. Then, without explanation, it goes out.

In January 2025, more than six years later, a photo of the dashboard shows it lit again.

Some lights are part of the car.

What follows is the life of that car with Tom. Close to eight years. California, Oregon and Nevada. More than 200,000 miles, and no one wrote down the last of them.

IThe Handshake · August 2018

31 Aug 2018 Evening, San Francisco. Purchase day.

The Handshake August 2018

A Craigslist ad, a test drive, and a full tank nobody asked for.

25 Aug 2018 Saturday, 25 August 2018, 10:18 in the morning. Tom answers a Craigslist ad on the Peninsula board.

The seller is Gil, a Mountain View dad whose new Tesla Model 3 has just arrived.

They meet at 7 pm in Mountain View, in front of the Wells Fargo.

After the test drive, Tom finds it in good shape. A few things are off: the window, several bumps and dings. The suspension worries him. Gil answers with a discount and a joke.

The price is $1,200, as is. Gil is honest about the sticky window.

Fri 31 Aug 2018 Friday, 31 August. Tom takes an Uber to Mountain View. Gil, on his way to meet him, stops at a gas station.

He fills the tank. He did not have to.

That evening he sends the service records too.

IIThe Naming · Late 2018

28 Dec 2018 Bodega Bay, golden hour. Six days after the first "beige stallion" email.

The Naming Late 2018

By Christmas, it had a name.

Two days after the purchase, it went to Mount Diablo.

Then it went out almost every weekend. Pacifica. Montara. Pescadero. San Juan Bautista. The redwoods at Boulder Creek and Big Basin.

When something broke, Tom fixed most of it himself, with cheap parts from Amazon.

In November, Gil mailed him the spare key.

22 Dec 2018 On 22 December 2018 the name appears in writing for the first time. Tom emails Nate a photo titled “Anne, Mike, tree, and beige stallion”. The message has no words in it. Most likely, the Christmas tree is riding on the car.

The Beige Stallion

Six days later, it stood on the shore at Bodega Bay in the last of the light.

19 Jan 2019 In January he sends Brian a photo under the subject “The beige stallion 2000 corolla”, with an offer: “Let me know when you want to trade.”

The Roads 2018 to 2022

California, Oregon and Nevada. Some of it certain, some of it likely, some of it possible.

In 2022 Tom told Gil he had taken the car “all over California, Oregon, and Nevada.”

Google Maps kept a monthly record from 2018 to 2024. From November 2019 a BMW wagon shared the road, so not every trip can be claimed. The map shows what is sure, what is very likely, and what is only possible.

      The Quiet Years 2020 to 2022

      Fewer roads, more repairs, and a sale ad that never ran.

      Apr 2020 April 2020. The car logs 356 miles in the month, mostly Pacifica and grocery runs to Daly City.

      Julia drafted the ad. Three months later he reworked it and added a Bluetooth adapter. No sale followed.

      V199,999 · October 2022

      199,999

      The odometer rolls over. The photo catches the mile before.

      13 Oct 2022 On or about 13 October 2022, the Corolla passed 200,000 miles. About 50,000 of them came after Tom bought it.

      At 12:53 in the morning, he wrote to Gil, with a photo of the odometer.

      October 2022 · 12:53 am

      “the Corolla hit 200 000 miles today! I've taken it all over California, Oregon, and Nevada and still have a ton of places to go.”

      “I still am grateful for you filling up that gas tank before selling me the car. You really didn't have to do that!”

      Tom, to Gil

      15 January 2023

      “Wow, it's amazing to see 199999!”

      Gil, to Tom

      He had meant to sell it. Instead he had put about $800 of parts into it, new tires included.

      Gil answered three months later, back in Mountain View, in the same house. He saw what the photo showed: 199,999, the last mile before the rollover.

      He suggested coffee or lunch, once the rain was over. Whether that coffee happened is not recorded.

      After this, the odometer goes quiet. The car kept going. No one wrote down how far.

      VIThe Last Autumn · 2024 to 2026

      31 Aug 2024 Angels Camp, Gold Country. Six years to the day after the purchase.

      The Last Autumn 2024 to 2026

      Door handles, sensors, and two trucks that never came.

      Jun 2024 June 2024. Still fixing it. Interior door handles, $12.37.

      31 Aug 2024 31 August 2024, six years to the day after the handshake, it stands under the pines at Angels Camp, in Gold Country.

      Nov 2024 Thanksgiving weekend 2024, it is in Big Sur, parked among the oaks.

      Dec 2024 December 2024. A 2006 Acura MDX joins the household. The Corolla stays on the policy.

      Aug 2025 August 2025. The Corolla is towed from a construction zone in San Francisco.

      Oct 2025 October 2025. An oxygen sensor and two kinds of cleaner arrive from Amazon. Possibly one last try at passing smog.

      Under Sutro Tower

      From October to Christmas Eve 2025, it is photographed where it is parked, under Sutro Tower. Again and again. By day, by streetlight, in the fog.

      October to Christmas Eve 2025

      Jul 2026 July 2026. An online scrap offer: $190. Tom accepts. The insurer asks for a final odometer reading. There is no sign one was sent.

      The first pickup is cancelled. A second buyer offers the same. The pickup is cancelled again.

      Then the emails stop. In mid-July 2026 it went to a scrap yard. Tom was paid $110 in cash.

      With the check-engine light fixed, it would have been worth about $3,000. He tried hard.

      The light that came on on the very first day was the one that ended it.

      It had one more ride in it. About two weeks later, a phone call: the yard had sold it to someone new, and the car had been totalled.

      There was no ceremony. This is the ceremony.

      VIICoda: The Chains · 2019 to 2026

      Coda: The Chains

      Bought for Tahoe. Taken to Tahoe. Never put on.

      Feb 2019 In February 2019, before the first snow trip, Tom ordered a set of KONIG CB-12 snow chains.

      Sep 2026 In September 2026, after the car was gone, he listed a set of CB-12 chains on Facebook Marketplace for $50.

      They had been to Tahoe once. They had never been mounted. They do not fit the new car's 18-inch wheels.

      Likely, they are the same chains.

      They never touched the road.

      In 2018 Tom said his next car would be electric. In 2026 he drives a Honda Civic Hybrid. Halfway there.
